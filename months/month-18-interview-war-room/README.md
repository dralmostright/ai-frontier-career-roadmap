# Month 18: Interview War Room

**Weeks 69-78 · Phase 6: Research Engineering and Interview Execution · Lab: `All labs — polish only`**

---

## The Month In One Sentence

Ten weeks, no new learning. Convert seventeen months of work into offers.

## Why This Month Exists

Everything is built. This month is conversion.

The failure mode to avoid is treating Month 18 as more learning. It is not. If
you reach Week 69 with material you have not learned, the honest move is to
extend Phase 5 or 6 rather than compress the conversion work — because an
underprepared application burns the referral and the company for six to twelve
months.

Ten weeks, in three blocks. Weeks 69-70 rebuild coding speed, which will have
decayed. Weeks 71-74 convert knowledge into fluent spoken explanation, which is a
separate skill from knowing. Weeks 75-78 are portfolio, mocks, applications, and
the final readiness call.

The thing most candidates get wrong: they prepare answers rather than practising
delivery. Recording yourself is uncomfortable and it is the highest-return
activity in the month.

## The Through-Lines

**Explanation is a separate skill from understanding.** You can implement
backpropagation and be unable to explain it. Reps fix this.

**Record everything.** Watch it back. Your self-assessment is whatever you would
give a stranger who sent you that recording.

**Lead with the moat.** Operational depth, the DBA agent, and the research. Not
the coursework.

**Fix one thing at a time.** After each mock, pick exactly one weakness. Trying to
fix seven fixes zero.

## The One Week Not To Compress

**Week 76.** Three full mock loops. Everything before it is preparation for it; everything after it responds to what it exposes.

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learning new material | Month 18 becomes months 18-24 | Conversion only. If material is missing, extend Phase 6 instead. |
| Preparing answers, not delivery | Fluent until the follow-up | Practice out loud, recorded, with follow-ups. |
| Skipping the recordings | No calibration | Watch them. It is unpleasant and it is the point. |
| Leading with coursework | Sounds like a bootcamp graduate | Lead with operational depth and the flagships. |
| Applying to top choices first | Burns the best opportunities on your rustiest loops | Second-tier first, for loop experience. |
| Trying to fix everything after a mock | Nothing improves | One thing per mock. |

## Advancement

Before Month 19, you should be able to, without notes:

- [ ] Solve a medium coding problem in 20 minutes, narrating, with a test
- [ ] Derive attention, backprop, and softmax+CE cold on a whiteboard
- [ ] Deliver six system designs in 45 minutes each with tradeoffs and failure modes
- [ ] Tell twelve STAR stories in 90-120 seconds each
- [ ] Present the portfolio in four minutes, 90 seconds, and 20 seconds
- [ ] Score 142+ on a full simulated loop

If two or more are shaky, take a catch-up week before advancing.
