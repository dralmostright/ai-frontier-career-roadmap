# Week 77: Application Strategy

## Outcome

By Sunday you have a target company matrix, referral paths mapped, outreach sent, and applications submitted in a deliberate order.

## Why This Matters For OpenAI/Anthropic-Level Interviews

Applying is a process with a strategy, and the most common mistake is applying
to your top choices first while you are still rusty.

Apply to second-tier targets first. Real loops are the only true calibration, and
the first two or three will go worse than your mocks. Burning your best
opportunities on those is expensive and avoidable.

Referrals matter disproportionately. A referred application gets read; a cold one
often does not. Map the paths you have — former colleagues, people you have
interacted with online, alumni networks — and ask specifically rather than
generally.

Tailor the pitch per company. Generic enthusiasm reads as unserious; naming their
actual work and connecting it to a specific flagship of yours does not.

## Theory Lessons

1. **Target selection**
   1. Tiers by desirability and reach
   2. Role types: AI Engineer, ML Engineer, LLM Platform, Research Engineer, AI Infrastructure
   3. Company types: frontier labs, AI-first startups, enterprise AI teams
   4. Which of your flagships each type cares about most
2. **Sequencing**
   1. Second tier first for loop experience
   2. Timing so offers overlap
   3. Why applying to everything at once is worse
3. **Referrals**
   1. Mapping your network honestly
   2. Asking specifically
   3. What makes a referral request easy to say yes to
4. **Tailoring**
   1. Naming their actual work
   2. Connecting a specific flagship to a specific problem they have
   3. The 60-second career-change narrative, adapted

## Stretch Goal

Reach out to five people currently in the roles you are targeting for short informational conversations. Most will say yes. They will tell you what they were actually asked, what their team is working on, and often whether they would refer you. It is the highest-information activity available and almost nobody does it.
